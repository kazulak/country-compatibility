How it works & limitations

Everything this site does, and everything it gets wrong, in one place.

1. What this is

Country Compass is a just-for-fun side project. You say what matters to you, and it ranks countries (and a list of well-known universities) by how well they match. It is not a research tool. Most numbers are hand-written guesses; seven metrics use real World Bank data.

Please don't use this for real decisions. For visas, safety and money, use official government portals, travel advisories and the institutions themselves.

2. How the score works

Each country has a 0–10 number for each of 32 metrics. A value comes from one of two sources: real World Bank data converted to 0–10 (7 metrics, see section 4), or a hand-made estimate (the other 25, and any World Bank gap). For every metric you set an importance, which becomes a weight:

  • Off = 0 (ignored)
  • Low = 3
  • Med = 6
  • High = 10

Each metric then gets a match score between 0 and 1, depending on its type:

  • Maximize metrics (higher is better): score = value / 10
  • Preference metrics (you pick a target): score = 1 − |target − value| / 10

The total is the weighted average of those scores, times 100, rounded:

total = 100 × sum(score × weight) / sum(weight)

Metrics that are Off, or that have no data for a country, are skipped rather than guessed. If every metric is Off, everything scores 100. If you turned metrics on but none of them has data for an item (only possible for Hong Kong universities, which have just a rank), it shows "n/a" and is listed last instead of scoring 100.

Worked example. You set Warm Weather (preference) to a target of 8 with High importance, and Safety (maximize) with Med importance. A country has warm weather 6 and safety 9.

  • Warm weather: 1 − |8 − 6| / 10 = 0.80, weight 10
  • Safety: 9 / 10 = 0.90, weight 6
  • Total: (0.80 × 10 + 0.90 × 6) / (10 + 6) = 13.4 / 16 = 0.8375, so 84
The model is simple and has not been validated against anything. It is linear, has no interactions between metrics, uses arbitrary weights (3/6/10), and "preference" metrics treat being over your target exactly the same as being under it (a country 2 points too hot scores the same as one 2 points too cold).

    6. Known problems and likely errors

    • Templates fit some countries badly. China, South Korea, Uzbekistan and Azerbaijan use the "Temperate Europe" template. Kenya, Nigeria and Ethiopia use "Tropical (Latin America)". The "Gulf / Desert" template claims zero income tax and high safety for Egypt, Algeria, Libya and others.
    • Template countries ignore current events. Their estimates do not reflect conflicts, travel advisories or sanctions.
    • Countries sharing a template have identical estimates for the 25 metrics without World Bank data, so they tie when only those metrics are switched on.
    • Visa rules change often. The visa text on curated countries may be out of date, and template visa text is generic.
    • Scores are integers from 0 to 10 written by one person. There is no second opinion and no inter-rater check.
    • Several universities share an approximate rank, and ranks beyond 100 are only bands.
    • University scores describe the country, not the university.
    • The quiz presets are arbitrary. They are convenient starting points, not findings.
    • Homicide rate ignores war, terrorism and petty crime, and the data can be years old. Ukraine's value is from 2021, before the war shows up (it scores about 6.0). Eleven countries' latest values are from 2015–2019, including Egypt (2017), Senegal and Turkmenistan (2015); the data table shows each year.
    • PM2.5 is a modelled national average, not what you breathe in a given city.
    • Internet measures access (share of people online), not speed.
    • GDP per capita is an average, not wages for your job. It is also inflated where multinationals book profits, so Ireland and Luxembourg look richer than local wages suggest.
    • The price level is economy-wide and misses expat rents in expensive cities. Singapore scores mid-range despite very high rents.
    • Life expectancy is a health outcome shaped by much more than healthcare quality.
    • Forest cover is not all nature: it ignores grassland, wetlands, mountains and desert landscapes, and includes plantations.
    • Taiwan has no World Bank data, and a few microstates miss some metrics. They fall back to the old estimates.
    • The 0–10 conversions use anchor points chosen by hand, which is itself a judgement call.
    • Labels of the hand-made metrics, such as "Happiness" or "Ease of Doing Business", do not match any official index.
